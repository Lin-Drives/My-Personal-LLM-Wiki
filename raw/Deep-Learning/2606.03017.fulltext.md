Abstract
Reward transfer from inverse reinforcement learning (IRL)
is unreliable when behavior must generalize to unseen com-
binations of task dynamics and goals. We present a method
for contrastive learning of factorized abstractions for trans-
ferable IRL that enables compositional reward transfer by
learning decoupled latent representations of dynamics and
goal. ConTraIRL uses a dual-encoder architecture to map
observations into separate dynamics and goal latent spaces,
optimized using two complementary contrastive objectives.
Phase-conditioned contrastive learning encourages the dy-
namics encoder to learn goal-invariant representations, with
an analogous contrastive term encouraging the goal encoder
tolearndynamics-invariantrepresentations.Thisfactorization
intodynamicsandgoalsupportsrewardrecoveryundernovel
combinations of dynamics and goals. Experiments on con-
tinuous control benchmarks and a simulated robotics testbed
demonstrate effective transfer to unseen dynamics-goal pair-
ings under limited target supervision, outperforming existing
transferable IRL and contrastive imitation learning methods.
1 Introduction
Inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) infers a reward func-
tion from observed expert behavior, enabling agents to ac-
quire complex skills without manual reward design (Arora
and Doshi 2021; Ho and Ermon 2016). Many IRL problems
contain multiple sources of variation, which we refer to as
factors. We focus on two types: a dynamics factor, which
determines how the environment changes in response to an
agent’s actions, and a goal factor, which specifies the behav-
ior the agent should achieve. Examples of dynamics factors
include friction and actuator capability, while goal factors
include reaching a location or maintaining a target veloc-
ity. We study compositional reward transfer, where source
demonstrations cover several dynamics and goal pairings,
but a target environment combines a dynamics setting and a
goal that have each been observed previously, but never to-
gether (Wiedemer et al. 2023; Lippl and Stachenfeld 2024).
Becauseeachexperttrajectoryreflectsbothfactors,areward
model learned jointly from the observed pairings without
separating their effects may encode dependencies specific
to those pairings and generalize poorly to an unseen pair-
ing (Gangwani and Peng 2020; Buening, Villin, and Dimi-
trakakis 2024; Schlaginhaufen and Kamgarpour 2024).

6 Conclusion
We introduced ConTraIRL for compositional reward trans-
fer to target contexts that combine previously observed dy-
namics and goal factors in a new pairing. Its key novelty is
factorized occupancy matching: for each phase, ConTraIRL
matches expert and learner occupancies separately for the
dynamicsandgoalfactors,ratherthanmatchingasingleoc-
cupancyfortheirjointcontext.Crossfactorinvarianceallows
eachabstractiontotransferacrosschangesintheotherfactor,
while phase consistency preserves behavioral progress. Our
analysis shows that minimizing the ConTraIRL objective is
equivalent to match the expert dynamics and goal occupan-
cies, enabling transfer to their unseen combination. Target
anchors provide additional calibration when the source and
target occupancies differ. Experiments across four MuJoCo
environments show that ConTraIRL consistently improves
performanceonunseencombinationsofdynamicsandgoals.
Ablations and probing experiments verify the contributions
of factorization, phase consistency, and target anchors.

Dynamics and goal construction.We construct the dynamics factors by varying which actuators remain functional. For
Ant, only the indicated one, two, or three legs remain functional. For HalfCheetah and Walker2d, only the indicated leg
remains functional, except in the full-functionality setting. For Swimmer, only joint 1, joint 2, or all joints remain functional.
HalfCheetah and Walker2d additionally distinguish walking and running as lower- and higher-speed behaviors under each
dynamics configuration. The goal factors specify the desired direction of motion. Ant uses forward, backward, leftward, and
rightward goals, while Swimmer uses forward and backward goals. The task rewards are used only to train expert policies and
report evaluation returns; they are not provided to the IRL methods.

C.4 Inferring Dynamics Labels
Our main experiments assume that each expert trajectory is annotated with its dynamics factor. In practice, expert trajectories
maybecollectedwithoutdynamicsannotationsevenwhenthesetofsourcedynamicsfactorsandtheirenvironmentsisknown.
We therefore evaluate whether each trajectory can be associated with its dynamics factor using states collected from the source
environments.
Because the dynamics variations in our benchmarks are defined by joint functionality, we use only the joint-related values
fromeachstateforlabelinference.Thisreducestheinfluenceofgoalandphaseinformation.Weapplyk-meanstothejoint-state
values from all source expert trajectories, setting the number of clusters to the known number of dynamics factors. Each state
receives a cluster label, and each trajectory is assigned to the cluster containing the majority of its states.
The cluster indices do not directly identify the corresponding dynamics factors. We therefore collect probe states from
each known dynamics environment using uniformly sampled random actions. Ak-nearest-neighbor classifier is trained on the
expert joint-state values using their cluster assignments as labels and then applied to the probe states. We enforce a one-to-one
correspondencebetweendynamicsfactorsandclustersbyselectingtheassignmentthatmaximizesthetotalnumberofmatching
probe-state predictions. This correspondence assigns a dynamics label to each expert trajectory.
The inferred trajectory labels achieve95.6%accuracy relative to the ground-truth dynamics labels, which are used only for
evaluation. The result indicates that joint-state occupancies are sufficiently distinct to recover dynamics labels in the evaluated

D Further Discussions
D.1 Connection to Zero-Shot Compositional Transfer
ConTraIRL considers compositional transfer with limited target supervision rather than zero-shot transfer. Source support
ensures that each target factor has appeared in the source contexts, but it does not ensure that source trajectories cover the
states induced by their target pairing. When the factor-conditioned target occupancy differs from the corresponding source
occupancies, source demonstrations alone cannot identify how target-specific states should be represented or rewarded. Target
anchorsaddressthisshiftbyaligningtargetstateswithsource-derivedexpertabstractionsforthesamefactorandphase,thereby
calibratingtherewardonthetargetoccupancy.Ifthesourceandtargetfactor-conditionedoccupanciescoincide,transferwithout
anchors may be possible; however, fully zero-shot transfer is outside the scope of this work.

D.3 Limitations
ConTraIRL has two main limitations. First, its contrastive objectives require repeatedly sampling expert–expert and expert–
learnerpairs.Moreover,becausetheencoderschangeduringtraining,thephase-conditionedexpectedexpertabstractionsmustbe
updated throughout optimization. These operations increase the computational cost as the numbers of contexts, factors, phases,
andcontrastivesamplesgrow.Moreefficientpairsampling,memorybanks,orrunningestimatesoftheexpertabstractionscould
reduce this cost.
Second, ConTraIRL assumes that phase labels are available and comparable across contexts. We do not develop a self-
supervised method for inferring phases from trajectories. This assumption may be restrictive for behaviors with variable
durations,nonmonotonicprogress,orcyclicstructure.Learningphaserepresentationsjointlywiththefactorizedabstractionsis
an important direction for future work.
